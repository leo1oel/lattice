# The source editor

One of the features [the GPUI app](../README.md) has so far.

A jump to a line (an error, the outline, SyncTeX, Go to line) flashes the whole line on the find-match colour, holds it and fades it over 1.6 s, as the Tauri app's `reveal-flash` does; under reduced motion it holds without fading; a jump into another editor takes the earlier editor's flash away at once.
The `attention` e2e module checks the flash comes and goes, and goes from the first editor when a jump lands in a second.

Edits sources in Zed's editor with LaTeX highlighting in Lattice's code themes, find and replace in the file ([Finding things](find.md)) and an optional Vim mode (Zed's Vim, with Lattice's own `:` command line at the editor's foot, where `:w` saves and builds as ⌘S does, `:q` closes the tab, `:wq` does both and `:N` goes to line N, as the Tauri app's CodeMirror Vim has them); the base keys where Zed's differ are CodeMirror's, as the Tauri app binds them (⌃L selects the line, recentering instead with the Emacs keymap on, ⌘I the enclosing syntax outside a `.tex` file, ⌥⌘\ re-indents, ⌃⌥[ and ⌃⌥] fold and unfold everything, and a ⌘-click adds a cursor; in a `.tex` file ⌃⌥[ and ⌥⌘[ fold the ranges the fold column folds, as the Tauri app's LaTeX fold service does); Format document (the palette's, in a `.tex` file) runs latexindent and applies its result only while the buffer still holds the text it was made from; in a `.tex` file, a card for the citation key or `\ref` label under the pointer, completion of commands, citation keys and labels, Bold and Emphasis (⌘B and ⌘I, putting `\textbf` or `\emph` on the selection or taking it off, as the Tauri app does), Go to definition (F12, or a ⌘-click, Ctrl-click off macOS: a `\ref`'s or `\label`'s label opens where it is defined, the open file's unsaved labels among the project's, a citation key its bibliography entry, an `\input` or `\include` the file the build reads and an `\includegraphics` the figure, paths read from the root document's folder and the `\graphicspath` roots as TeX reads them, and anything else is asked of TexLab when it is installed), the editing menu on a right-click (Cut, Copy, Paste and Select All, as WebKit's menu offers them over the Tauri app's source, the caret put at the pointer when it lies outside the selection), the Tauri app's typing rules (`$` pairing, or wrapping the selection in `$…$`, Enter after a `\begin{…}` writing its `\end{…}`, Enter on an `\item` line starting the next item or leaving the list from an empty one, and a typed or completed `\begin{…}` or `\left(` closed by its `\end{…}` or `\right)`, with Vim on only in its insert mode), Insert citation and Insert reference (⌘⇧K and ⌘⇧L, a picker of the bibliography's keys with title, byline and year, or of the project's labels, unsaved ones in the open file among them, writing `\cite{…}` or `\ref{…}` at the caret), the source's own lint, as the Tauri app runs it beside TexLab's 400 ms after the last edit and again when the project's index is read again (unmatched and unclosed environments, unclosed math, duplicate labels and bibliography keys, an `\input` or figure the project does not have, with Create file on its card, or has only in another letter case, with Fix case, and, once the index has landed, unknown citation keys and labels, a label another file defines too, and, without TexLab to report them, unused ones; `%novalidate` exempts a line and `%begin novalidate` … `%end novalidate` a block), and TexLab's diagnostics (when `texlab` is installed), through Lattice's editor adapter (below).
The source gutter is the Tauri app's: four-digit line numbers and a fold column whose chevrons show over the gutter and fold a LaTeX environment, section, preamble, brace group or comment block, so the text starts where CodeMirror's does.
A `.tex` file has the outline rail on the editor's right edge (its sections' marks, the one being read in the accent; resting on it opens the outline with numbers and PDF pages, and a row jumps there), and the Source / Visual switch in its strip (its ⋯ menu repeats it), one choice for every `.tex` file, shared by every window and remembered, as the Tauri app's is.
Visual is the Tauri app's Visual mode, its CodeMirror source editor drawn as a page (headings, emphasis, maths through KaTeX, lists, figures, tables, references and citations as chips, the preamble folded), as a webview island in the source's place over the panel's buffer: an edit lands in the file as the change it made and nothing else, undo and redo are the buffer's, so either view steps back through the other's edits, grouped as the page's own history groups them (a word typed in one go is one ⌘Z, as in the Tauri app, however its keystrokes reach the buffer), the caret is the status bar's and crosses the switch by line, a jump to a line lands in it, and a ⌘-click on a reference, citation or `\input` opens it. Its ⌘⇧E and ⌘⌥R ask for the name in the window's dialog, which the page then applies at its own selection, as the Tauri app's canvas does.
Find (⌘F and the strip's Find) opens the page's own search panel there, and an `\input` of an editable figure's entry file draws the figure.
The outline rail stands over the page's right edge as it does over the source (the Tauri app's own rail, its marks, the outline with numbers and PDF pages, and a row's jump showing the same place in the PDF).
Spelling and grammar are checked there as in the source (Harper, or a language's dictionary, with the project's dictionary and ignores, and a card's Add to dictionary and Ignore writing to the manifest), TexLab's completion, hovers and Go to definition join its own when TexLab is installed, and without TexLab the labels and citation keys nothing uses are marked.
⇧F12 and F2 on a label or citation key open the window's references bar and rename dialog, and an image pasted there goes in as a figure at the page's caret, which the source's follows.
The project's local annotations are marked there as in the source, each passage tinted by its status with its numbered pin in the gutter; a pin opens its note in the Annotations panel, and the card over a passage resolves it or takes a reply. Co-authors' Overleaf carets are drawn there with their names.
In a linked Overleaf project the file's comment threads are marked there in their openers' colours, and margin review shows them beside the text as the source's does (the Tauri app's own layer: cards right of the text with room for the measure, else pips, as the palette's Margin review mode says), its cards replying, resolving, deleting and editing messages through the window's Overleaf bridge; Overleaf's tracked changes are marked there as in the source, with the same card under the pointer and their cards in margin review, Accept and Reject going to Overleaf through the bridge.
What it does not have yet is in [Not yet](not-yet.md).
It reads the project's citations and labels from the index the source's cards read, its TeX sources for macros and `\graphicspath`, its images through the island's asset protocol, and the last build's `.aux` for the numbers it draws.

## In the e2e scenario

In `main.tex` it checks the editor adapter: the citation and reference cards open when native mouse moves rest on a key or label and close when the pointer leaves, remote carets draw and name themselves and fade, a block widget pushes the lines below it down, a gutter mark set on a line is painted there and opens its card, TexLab's diagnostic for an unknown `\ref` shows as a mark whose card opens and clears once the reference goes, and with the bibliography unlinked, TexLab's diagnostic on the cited key opens its lint card under the citation card (both skipped without `texlab`), a card opens over its range's last character and not from the space past its line's end, a `.tex` file renamed to `.txt` is never synced to TexLab, and native keys complete a citation key, `\textbf`, and a package in the middle of `\usepackage`'s list (prefix matches first, the bare name inserted). In a chapter (`scenario/e2e/latex_keys.rs`), ⌘B / Ctrl+B starts no build, ⌘B and ⌘I put `\textbf` and `\emph` on a selected word and take them off again, ⌘⇧M makes it inline math, ⌘⌥E wraps it in an equation that ⌘⌥A then selects, ⌘/ comments its line, ⌘⌥U upper-cases it, ⌘⇧E wraps it in the environment named and ⌘⌥R renames that environment's delimiters, and ⌘⇧K and ⌘⇧L open their insert pickers and leave the text and the selection as they were. Typed with native keys (`scenario/e2e/latex_typing.rs`), `\begin{itemize}` is closed by its `\end{itemize}` with the caret on its first `\item`, ↩ on an item starts the next and on the empty one leaves the list, `$` writes a pair, `\left(` grows its `\right)`, and ⌃M in that math selects its opening `$`. In `main.tex` (`scenario/e2e/latex_definition.rs`, macOS), F12 on `\ref{sec:method}` lands on its `\label`'s line, a native ⌘-click on `\cite{knuth1984}` opens `references.bib` at its entry, F12 on `\input{chapters/intro}` opens the chapter, and a native right-click on a word puts the caret there and opens the editing menu, which keeps the keyboard until Escape gives it back to the text. The editor's own commands (`scenario/e2e/editor_commands.rs`, macOS): ⌃L selects the caret's line, a native ⌘-click on plain text adds a cursor, Format document indents a scratch file's items with latexindent (skipped without it), and with Vim on, `:` opens the command line, where `:w` saves the file, `:3` goes to line 3 and `:q` closes the tab. The lint (`scenario/e2e/index_lint.rs`, macOS), on a scratch file: an unknown `\ref` is underlined and the fixture's own label is not, a native pointer resting on it opens the lint card, a missing `\input` offers Create file, which makes the file and opens it, one in another letter case offers Fix case, which respells it, and an environment left open is an error. The pickers (`scenario/e2e/insert_pickers.rs`): ⌘⇧K lists the bibliography's keys and writes `\cite{knuth1984}` at the caret, ⌘⇧L lists the labels, one typed in the chapter and not yet saved among them, and writes `\ref{sec:method}`, ⌘G, ⌘⇧K and ⌘⇧L each switch straight to their own dialog over another and close it on their own key again, ⌘G goes to the line typed in Go to line, and ⌘⇧O lists the sections and labels, an unsaved label among them, and opens the label picked.
With `LATTICE_E2E_SHOTS=DIR` (macOS) it saves the window at each card, the carets and the completion menus.

In `e2e-visual.tex` (`scenario/e2e/latex_visual.rs`, macOS) it chooses Visual and checks the page drawing the folded preamble, the section as a heading, the bold run, KaTeX's maths, the list's bullets and the citation chip; that the outline rail stands over the page with its sections' marks, the spell check marks a misspelt word there, and a local annotation is marked and pinned there until it is resolved; that opening writes nothing; that a word typed there with native keys is saved as exactly that word and one ⌘Z typed there undoes all of it through the buffer, its space typed after the buffer's own grouping interval; that text put into the buffer reaches the page; that ⌘⇧E typed in the page asks for the environment in the window's dialog and wraps the page's selection in it, and ⌘⌥R renames both its delimiters to the name typed, the keyboard back in the page; and that choosing Source again hides the page.
