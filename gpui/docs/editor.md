# The source editor

One of the features [the GPUI app](../README.md) has so far.

Edits sources in Zed's editor with LaTeX highlighting in Lattice's code themes, find and replace in the file ([Finding things](find.md)) and an optional Vim mode (Zed's Vim); in a `.tex` file, a card for the citation key or `\ref` label under the pointer, completion of commands, citation keys and labels, Bold and Emphasis (⌘B and ⌘I, putting `\textbf` or `\emph` on the selection or taking it off, as the Tauri app does), and TexLab's diagnostics (when `texlab` is installed), through Lattice's editor adapter (below).
The source gutter is the Tauri app's: four-digit line numbers and a fold column whose chevrons show over the gutter and fold a LaTeX environment, section, preamble, brace group or comment block, so the text starts where CodeMirror's does.
A `.tex` file has the outline rail on the editor's right edge (its sections' marks, the one being read in the accent; resting on it opens the outline with numbers and PDF pages, and a row jumps there), and the Source / Visual switch in its strip (its ⋯ menu repeats it), one choice for every `.tex` file, shared by every window and remembered, as the Tauri app's is.
Visual is the Tauri app's Visual mode, its CodeMirror source editor drawn as a page (headings, emphasis, maths through KaTeX, lists, figures, tables, references and citations as chips, the preamble folded), as a webview island in the source's place over the panel's buffer: an edit lands in the file as the change it made and nothing else, undo and redo are the buffer's, so either view steps back through the other's edits, the caret is the status bar's and crosses the switch by line, a jump to a line lands in it, and a ⌘-click on a reference, citation or `\input` opens it.
Find (⌘F and the strip's Find) opens the page's own search panel there, and an `\input` of an editable figure's entry file draws the figure.
What it does not have yet is in [Not yet](not-yet.md).
It reads the project's citations and labels from the index the source's cards read, its TeX sources for macros and `\graphicspath`, its images through the island's asset protocol, and the last build's `.aux` for the numbers it draws.

## In the e2e scenario

In `main.tex` it checks the editor adapter: the citation and reference cards open when native mouse moves rest on a key or label and close when the pointer leaves, remote carets draw and name themselves and fade, a block widget pushes the lines below it down, a gutter mark set on a line is painted there and opens its card, TexLab's diagnostic for an unknown `\ref` shows as a mark whose card opens and clears once the reference goes, and with the bibliography unlinked, TexLab's diagnostic on the cited key opens its lint card under the citation card (both skipped without `texlab`), a card opens over its range's last character and not from the space past its line's end, a `.tex` file renamed to `.txt` is never synced to TexLab, and native keys complete a citation key, `\textbf`, and a package in the middle of `\usepackage`'s list (prefix matches first, the bare name inserted). In a chapter (`scenario/e2e/latex_keys.rs`), ⌘B / Ctrl+B starts no build, ⌘B and ⌘I put `\textbf` and `\emph` on a selected word and take them off again, and ⌘⇧K and ⌘⇧L leave the text and the selection as they were.
With `LATTICE_E2E_SHOTS=DIR` (macOS) it saves the window at each card, the carets and the completion menus.

In `e2e-visual.tex` (`scenario/e2e/latex_visual.rs`, macOS) it chooses Visual and checks the page drawing the folded preamble, the section as a heading, the bold run, KaTeX's maths, the list's bullets and the citation chip; that opening writes nothing; that a word typed there with native keys is saved as exactly that word and ⌘Z typed there undoes it through the buffer; that text put into the buffer reaches the page; and that choosing Source again hides the page.
