# App updater

One of the features [the GPUI app](../README.md) has so far.

Updates the app as the Tauri app's updater does (`src/telemetry/app-updater.tsx`). The GPUI binary ships as two apps (`crates/lattice-gpui/src/flavor.rs`, read from the bundle's identifier), and each updates from its own feed:

- **Lattice**, the native app (`app.leo1oel.researchwriter`, the Tauri app's identity, on Apple silicon Macs whose writer chose it) follows the native feed, `latest-native.json`, always, as the Tauri app follows its own. Settings › Editor & builds shows the Version row with Check for updates, and "Switch back to the classic version".
- **Lattice Beta** (`app.leo1oel.lattice-beta`, beside the Tauri app) follows its own channel and ships off: it does nothing until the writer turns on Settings › Editor & builds › Beta updates (`appUpdates` in the settings file, off by default). Until then the updater checks nothing, Lattice ▸ Check for Updates… is not in the menu, the Version row links to GitHub Releases, and Report a problem files the report at once. A binary outside a bundle (a build tree, the e2e) is Lattice Beta.

While it is on, it reads the release manifest (`latest.json`, in the shape the Tauri app's updater reads) at once, then at launch and every six hours, and offers a newer release for this platform ("New version …", Update now); the Version row's Check for updates checks at once.
Update now downloads the archive with its progress, checks its minisign signature against the channel's key, and puts the app bundle it holds in place of the running one, unpacked beside it first so the swap is two renames; a failure says why ("Lattice update failed") and keeps the running app.
Once installed, Update now restarts into the release, asking first while a build runs ("Restart Lattice now?"); every window writes its open edits as the app quits.
Lattice ▸ Check for Updates… checks at once and says when Lattice is up to date, and becomes Restart to Update… once an update is installed.
Report a problem offers a release the updater knows of first ("You’re on vX; vY is available", Update now or Report anyway).

## Lattice: the native app, by the writer's choice

Every release ships both apps for Apple silicon, and moving between them is the writer's choice, in either direction. The design note is `handoffs/LATA-45/design.md` in the Captain drive. In short:

- **Identity:** `scripts/gpui-bundle.mjs --flavor main` builds `Lattice.app` with the Tauri app's identifier and Developer ID. Either updater unpacks the other app's archive over the running `Lattice.app` and restarts into it (the Tauri app relaunches the executable the new `Info.plist` names; the native app's `cx.restart()` reopens the bundle), so macOS privacy grants, Keychain items (one service for both apps) and the Dock place carry over.
- **Switching:** the Tauri app offers the native app on Apple silicon only, and only when the native feed answers with a `darwin-aarch64` entry; the native app's "Switch back to the classic version" installs the Tauri app from the classic feed. Both apps share version numbers (0.19.0 and 0.19.0), so a switch installs whatever version the other feed offers, through the same signature check as an update, and restarts into it.
- **Switching back:** Settings › Editor & builds › Switch back to the classic version, in Lattice on a Mac only (`updater::switch_to_classic`). It asks first, saying the classic version opens with its data as it was before the switch and that changes made in the native app don't flow back. Then it reads the Tauri app's feed (`plugins.updater.endpoints[0]` in `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`, or `LATTICE_CLASSIC_UPDATER_ENDPOINT` for a rehearsal), takes its `darwin-aarch64` release whatever its version, downloads it with its progress, checks its signature against the same key, puts it in place of this bundle and restarts into it, asking first while a build runs, as an update does. A failure says why ("Couldn’t switch to the classic version") and keeps the running app.
- **Data:** the native app keeps its own folder, `~/Library/Application Support/app.leo1oel.lattice` (and the same name under Caches), and never writes the Tauri app's. Its first launch carries the Tauri app's state over (`lattice_settings::migrate`, once, read-only on the Tauri app's folder, marked by `tauri-migration.json`) and announces the update (What's new). A later switch keeps the native app's own state and only merges the Tauri app's recent projects into its own (by path, newest first, never dropping its own). Nothing flows back: switching back finds the Tauri app's folder as it left it. Lattice Beta's `lattice-gpui` is left alone.
- **Feeds:** `.github/workflows/release.yml` builds both apps for every release. The Tauri app's files and `latest.json` are tauri-action's, on every platform, as before. The `publish` job adds the native app's files under names of their own, `Lattice-native_<version>_aarch64.dmg`, `.app.tar.gz` and `.app.tar.gz.sig`, and its manifest, `latest-native.json`, after `scripts/check-native-feed.mjs` checks the archive's signature against the updater key and that the manifest names this tag's archive; a release whose native app is missing or fails the check stays a draft. Intel Macs, Windows and Linux never see the native files (`macos-intel.yml` and the others edit only `latest.json`).

## The channels

- **Lattice's feed (native):** `https://github.com/leo1oel/lattice/releases/latest/download/latest-native.json`, an asset of every release beside the Tauri app's `latest.json`. The Tauri app reads it to offer the switch (`LATTICE_NATIVE_UPDATER_ENDPOINT` overrides it there), and the native app updates from it.
- **The classic feed:** `https://github.com/leo1oel/lattice/releases/latest/download/latest.json`, the Tauri app's (`plugins.updater.endpoints` in `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`). The native app reads it only to switch back (`LATTICE_CLASSIC_UPDATER_ENDPOINT` overrides it). Its release is installed whatever its version, but only with a signature the updater key accepts.
- **Lattice Beta's feed:** `https://github.com/leo1oel/lattice/releases/download/lattice-beta/latest.json`, an asset of one prerelease tagged `lattice-beta` that only Lattice Beta builds go to. A prerelease is never GitHub's latest release, so the Tauri app's `releases/latest/download/latest.json` never sees it, and its tag matches neither `release.yml` nor `gpui.yml` (`v*`).
- **Another feed:** `LATTICE_GPUI_UPDATER_ENDPOINT` names a manifest to read instead of the app's own feed (an `http(s)` or `file://` URL), for rehearsing a release against a local feed. What it offers is still checked against the updater key and, as an update, installed only when newer.
- **Signatures:** the Tauri updater's scheme and key: minisign over the `.app.tar.gz`, made by `tauri signer sign` with `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY`, checked against `plugins.updater.pubkey` in `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`, which the app reads at build time.
- **Versions:** the app's version is `lattice-core`'s, which follows the Tauri app's (`scripts/bump-version.mjs`), and the manifest carries the same number, so a GPUI build is offered only a newer release as an update. A switch between the two apps accepts any version, since both ship the same numbers.
- **Packages:** `../../scripts/gpui-bundle.mjs [--flavor beta|main]` builds `Lattice Beta.app` or `Lattice.app`, signs and notarizes it, and writes the `.dmg`, `.zip`, `Lattice-Beta_<version>_aarch64.app.tar.gz` (or `Lattice-native_<version>_aarch64.app.tar.gz`) with its `.sig`, and the flavor's manifest, `latest.json` (or `latest-native.json`, listing the archive under `darwin-aarch64` and `darwin-aarch64-app`), pointing at the archive on the feed. The bundle inside is `Lattice Beta.app` or `Lattice.app` either way. `.github/workflows/gpui-package.yml` makes them with the release secrets and keeps them as the run's artifact; it publishes nothing. It runs by hand (either flavor) or from `release.yml` (Lattice, on every release).
- **Publishing Lattice Beta (by hand, after a hand test of the artifact):** create the prerelease once (`gh release create lattice-beta --prerelease --title "Lattice Beta channel" --notes …`), then for each build upload the archive and its `.sig` first and `latest.json` last, `--clobber`, so a check never reads a manifest whose archive is not there yet.

## In the e2e scenario

`scenario/e2e/updater.rs`: the updater off, with no menu item; then pointed at a channel on disk with Beta updates turned on, a check offering the newer release and adding Check for Updates…, Report a problem offering it first, and Update now checking the archive's signature and saying why it could not install it outside an app bundle.
