# Overleaf

One of the features [the GPUI app](../README.md) has so far.

Syncs a project with Overleaf as the Tauri app does, through lattice-core's Overleaf client: Connect signs in on Overleaf's own login page in a window of its own (a private webview whose session cookie becomes Lattice's, the password never seen), and "Open from Overleaf" (the titlebar's project menu, the palette) lists the account's projects with search, the archived ones behind a checkbox, a pasted project or share link, pending invitations, and the folder already on this Mac for a project (one this app linked, one the Tauri app's list of linked folders names wherever it sits, or the project's download folder); Open opens that folder and syncs it there, else downloads one into `~/Documents/Overleaf Projects` (with the Tauri app's progress line) and opens it in a window of its own, or links the open project, which can also be uploaded as a new Overleaf project.
A linked project gets the titlebar's split Overleaf control (sync, with its status dot, and Open on Overleaf). In Live mode it pulls a co-author's work on its own and pushes an edit made here once the writer pauses; in Manual mode a badge says Overleaf has new work and Sync opens Review changes, the files that would change and each one's diff, before applying them. An edit on both sides of a line leaves conflict markers and opens the conflict resolver with this computer's and Overleaf's sides, a file deleted here asks before it goes on Overleaf (or as Settings says), and nothing uploads while markers remain.
With the live channel up, Live mode edits the open document live, on lattice-core's port of Overleaf's OT engine (`overleaf_rt::ot`): a co-author's typing lands in the buffer as they type, out of the undo history, and typing here goes to Overleaf a quarter of a second after the first unsent key, with Overleaf's document hash now and then so a drifted copy is refused instead of diverging; a document whose update is unanswered when the channel drops is joined again from its version once it is back, so the update is neither lost nor applied twice, and anything that cannot be proven goes back to regular sync.
The titlebar shows who else is in the project, with a hover card for each, and their carets in the editor; the status bar's save slot says whether the file reached Overleaf ("Saved and synced", "Saved, syncing…", "Saved on this Mac").
While presence reconnects the faces stay but the carets go, and a jump to someone in no file says so.
Live mode asks Overleaf whether its history moved every 3 s while the channel is down, every 45 s (with 30 s between syncs) once it carries documents, and nothing from a window off screen unless an edit of ours is owed; coming back to the window asks at once, and a channel waiting out its backoff joins then too and says "Overleaf live editing reconnected." when it does.
A board, sheet or deck, whose editor rewrites the whole file, is held from Overleaf while it is edited and goes up once the writer leaves it, the window loses focus, ⌘S saves, or a minute passes.
A live update whose answer is lost holds its file out of regular sync, with a notice naming it, until Overleaf's replay shows whether it landed.
The control's dot is green whenever the link reaches Overleaf (a sync, live editing, or the channel up), its icon fades up into place between linked and syncing, and its tooltip says when the file in front cannot be edited live.
Every sync is one operation in the app's log, a quiet no-op or a cancelled one included, with what it pulled, pushed and merged.
File ▸ Open from Overleaf… opens the picker, whose list Home and End also walk; View ▸ Margin Review offers Auto, Markers Only and Off for a linked source, and the palette's Review here opens the comment or suggestion by the caret.
Review changes keeps the list's selection on the file whose diff is at the top as the diffs scroll, and a file clicked in the list scrolls its diff smoothly into place.
Review follows the Tauri app's: a comment's passage is marked in its author's colour, a suggested insertion underlined in its author's hue and a suggested deletion's text struck through at its point, with a card over a suggestion to accept or reject it; the collaboration panel (the titlebar's comments button, the messages mark with what is waiting counted after it, opens it beside the document in a tab named Overleaf by its leaf) lists every thread by file with replies, edits, resolving and deleting, and the open file's suggestions under their sections with Accept and Reject one by one or all; Annotate selection starts a thread; margin review shows the threads and suggestions beside their lines, as cards right of the text when the editor has room for the measure and a card, stacked under one another with room under the last line to scroll the last of them into view, else as pips that open their cards under their line (above it near the view's bottom) so the passage stays in sight, and in a narrow editor as the rail (the palette's Margin review: Auto, Markers only, Off). Typing goes out as suggestions when Overleaf's track changes is on for the account, as Overleaf sets it.
The collaboration panel's Chat tab holds the project's chat: the conversation read from Overleaf when first shown and kept current on the live channel, each run of messages under its author's face and the day's name where the day changes, an unread count on the tab and in the titlebar's while the chat is not shown, and a composer that sends on Enter and keeps its text when Overleaf refuses it; the presence card's "Message everyone in Chat" opens it.
The History panel's Overleaf view (and its Everything stream) lists Overleaf's own history, every edit made in the browser included: who and where it came from, a version's names, each opened to its changed files and their diffs or compared with now, with a file restored, a deleted one brought back, the whole project rewound (asked first), and a version named or unnamed; a restore changes Overleaf's copy, which the project here then syncs.
Settings › Overleaf says who is signed in and which project this one syncs with, pauses and resumes it, reconnects an expired session or disconnects, and holds the sync mode and the remote-delete choice, imported from the Tauri app's.
The session and the list of linked folders live in the GPUI app's own data folder (`lattice-gpui/overleaf` in the system's data directory, or `LATTICE_GPUI_OVERLEAF_DIR`), never the Tauri app's, so signing in or out here leaves the Tauri app's session alone.
The Tauri app's list of linked folders (`overleaf-local-copies.json` in its config folder) is only read, never written, and each folder it names is still checked against its own link.
