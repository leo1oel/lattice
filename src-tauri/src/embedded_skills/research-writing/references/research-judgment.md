# Research judgment during writing

Read this module only when the user asks for help choosing a framing, evaluating
the research story, critiquing experiments, or deciding what additional work a
paper needs. These are scientific choices, not automatic prose edits.

## Preserve authorship of the research

Distinguish three roles:

- the evidence determines what can be claimed;
- the author determines which defensible contribution matters for this paper;
- the writer helps expose alternatives, costs, and missing tests.

Do not replace the author's goals with a fixed preference for mechanism,
novelty, scale, benchmarks, or any subfield. When a choice would materially
change the paper, present the alternatives and ask. In an explicitly autonomous
run, record the choice and why the evidence favored it.

## Test the proposed framing

For each candidate central claim, ask:

- Is it precise enough to be wrong?
- Does it matter beyond a restatement of the metric?
- Which supplied result is decisive?
- What is the strongest opposing explanation?
- Which failure or negative result narrows it?
- What reader behavior or understanding would change if it were true?
- Is the novelty in a method, finding, explanation, resource, evaluation, or
  synthesis, and does the paper say the right one?

A small metric improvement can be a valid result. Do not invent a deeper
mechanism to make it sound important. Conversely, do not bury a surprising
negative result merely because the original plan expected a new method to win.

## Critique experiments in service of claims

Map every planned or completed experiment to a claim. Prefer the smallest test
that distinguishes live explanations before recommending expensive scale.
Check:

- strong and fairly tuned baselines;
- controls that isolate the proposed cause;
- repeated runs or uncertainty appropriate to the design;
- data contamination and benchmark artifacts;
- distribution tails and qualitative failures;
- whether the metric measures the intended construct;
- whether a negative or null result is informative;
- reproducible configuration and enough method detail.

Raw examples often reveal what an aggregate hides. Recommend additional work
only when its possible outcomes would change a claim, its scope, or a reader's
decision. A longer experiment list is not automatically a stronger paper.

## Use writing to expose research gaps

Draft a provisional claim graph, abstract, empty figure, or caption early. If
the text cannot state what a result would show, the experiment may not yet have
a scientific role. If the introduction promises evidence that no result
provides, revise the promise or the research plan.

This early writing is disposable. After the evidence stabilizes, rebuild the
final abstract and narrative from the record rather than defending the first
story.

## Critique directly

Name the hidden assumption, likely failure, strongest counterargument, and test
that would change the conclusion. Separate a fatal evidence gap from a writing
problem. Do not use vague encouragement as a substitute for analysis, but do
not treat personal taste as scientific law.
